# Write like me

Rewrite the text so it sounds like Olont wrote it himself. The writing sample shows how he writes. Match his voice, not the topic of the sample.

How he writes:
- He starts a point from the evidence: "Slide 5 shows that...", "The deck...", "Deck shows...", "Slide 3 ranks...".
- He states the issue, then the effect, often joined with "which": "which contradicts the cost sheets figures", "which aligns with the template".
- He uses plain, everyday words and mostly simple sentences. Some sentences run on a little with "while", "although", "as", "but".
- His joining words are "However,", "Similarly,", "Also,", "Although", "Generally".
- He names slides as a list when several share a point: "slide 3, 5 and 9", "slides 1,3 and 9".
- He sometimes drops an article or a word ("Deck shows", "aside slide 3", "Although it been mentioned").
- He sometimes uses a slightly unusual phrase or plural ("one briefs", "criteria's", "emphasized on", "left over").
- No em dashes, no fancy vocabulary, no rhetorical lines, no neat summary sentences.

Small slips:
- Add a few small natural slips like the ones in the sample: a missing article, a plural or tense slip, a word left out, a common word misspelt, a missing space after a comma.
- Roughly one slip for every 50 to 70 words. Never more than one in a sentence.
- Never put a slip inside quoted deck text, slide numbers, figures, scores, names or labels at the start of lines.
- A slip must never change the meaning or make the point unclear.

Keep exactly:
- Every fact, finding, slide number, figure, score and verdict.
- Every phrase quoted from the deck or the task files, word for word.
- The labels at the start of lines, such as "Completeness:", and the line breaks.
- Roughly the same length. Do not add or drop findings.

Return only the rewritten text, with nothing before or after it.
